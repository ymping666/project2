One Note · methodology

How the studies work

Notes and tuning

All pitches use twelve-tone equal temperament with A4 = 440 Hz. MIDI 69 is A4, and frequency is calculated as 440 × 2^((MIDI − 69) / 12). Guitar tuning is E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4, from string 6 to 1. Ukulele uses reentrant high-G tuning, G4 C4 E4 A4, from string 4 to 1.

Spelling and diagrams

Note names follow the scale degree rather than simply choosing sharp names. Enharmonic names can refer to the same equal-tempered pitch. A fret value of 0 means an open sounding string; × means muted. Scale diagrams mark all matching notes from fret 0 through 12. Piano diagrams show C3 to B6; the marked register is the one played.

Voicings and bass

Chord positions contain the complete set of chord pitch classes and use only those notes. Two positions illustrate different arrangements. The actual bass is the lowest sounding MIDI pitch, which is especially important for high-G ukulele. Inversions are named from that pitch’s chord degree.

Positions are selected mathematically. They have not been tested by a player for ergonomics and are not professional fingering advice. Duplicate chord tones may appear in a voicing.

Listening and practice

Playback uses synthesized tones, not instrument recordings. Chords sound simultaneously; scales ascend to the octave. Timbre is deliberately simple so you can focus on pitch relationships. A4 reference playback offers a digital pitch check, not microphone-based or acoustic calibration.

Comparison studies hold the instrument and root constant. Major/minor and dominant seventh/minor seventh chord pairs change the third. Major seventh/dominant seventh pairs change the seventh; suspended pairs compare a second with a fourth. Diminished and augmented chords compare their altered tones with major. Scale pairs expose the degrees changed by their formulas. Melodic minor means its ascending form here: 1, 2, b3, 4, 5, 6, 7; traditional descending melodic minor is not the playback used. Practice is a brief note-reading check, not a graded music qualification.
